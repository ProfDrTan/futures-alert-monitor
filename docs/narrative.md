## 2026-09-24 (Thursday) -- 10Y at a 19-year high: talking points for Monday's class

10-year Treasury yield ~5.11-5.12%, near its highest level since
**2007**, having briefly touched 5% for the first time since then
before a slight pullback. Drivers, in order of weight:

1. **Strong US data** -- S&P Global PMI showed private-sector activity
   expanding at its fastest pace in 5+ years (services + manufacturing
   both improving), employment growing at its fastest pace since 2022.
2. **Rate-hike repricing** -- traders now price ~60% odds of another
   25bp Fed hike next month, up from under 10% a month ago.
3. **Iran conflict** -- hawkish remarks from Iranian leaders dimmed
   near-term diplomacy hopes, keeping energy prices elevated and
   adding inflation pressure.
4. **Weak Treasury auction demand** -- the latest 5-year note auction
   tailed 3.1bps above its 6-auction average, soft indirect (foreign)
   bidder participation; heavy AI-related corporate debt issuance
   ($1.5T+ this year) is competing for the same buyer pool.

**Market read-through:** this yield pressure is the same force behind
the Dow/Nasdaq divergence seen Sept 22-23 -- cyclical/refinancing-
exposed sectors (financials, industrials) absorb the damage, while
AI-heavy tech shrugs it off since that capex is contracted years
forward and largely rate-insensitive. Also the headwind on gold.
Some commentary (Fortune) is drawing 1970s-stagflation comparisons
given the yield's 2007-level breach -- worth presenting as a live
debate to students, not a settled call.

## 2026-09-21 (Monday) -- Nasdaq record close, +2.2%, against the seasonal grain

AI stocks surged back after last week's AI-bubble warnings faded, with
investors refocusing on continued AI infrastructure spend. Meta led
at +11-12% (best day since April 2025) on a Wells Fargo price-target
hike to $796 citing Meta's AI agent/coding tools ahead of its Sept
23-24 Connect event; Intel and AMD also jumped ~10-12%, AMD briefly
touching a $1T market cap. Oil dropped 5%+ (worst day in a month) on
signs of Middle East de-escalation, pulling Treasury yields down with
it -- removing the inflation-fear headwind that had been pressuring
stocks.

**Seasonality context:** this happened *against* the historical grain
-- September is the weakest month for US equities on average, and the
futures-alert-monitor system's own NQ data shows NQ has closed lower
in 6 of the last 16 Septembers (avg -1.11% for the month). A 2%+ up
day overriding that seasonal pattern is a stronger signal than the
same move in a seasonally favorable month. Also in play: Q3-end
"window dressing" -- fund managers rotating into winners like AI names
ahead of quarterly reports, a mechanical effect that can unwind just
as fast once the quarter turns.

## 2026-09-17

Markets snapped back sharply the day after the Fed's Wednesday rate
hike -- Kevin Warsh's first as chair, and the first hike in three
years. The Dow rose 316 points (0.6%) to 51,778.04, the S&P 500 gained
1.14% to 7,637.76, and the Nasdaq jumped 1.69% to 26,418.30, with nine
of eleven S&P sectors green and the move led by big tech (Nvidia,
Amazon, Microsoft, Intel, AMD).

Two things drove the reversal. First, oil kept falling -- US crude
dropped another 2.5%, below $100/barrel, extending the prior
session's 3%+ decline. Second, and more important: Warsh's press
conference itself. His earlier Jackson Hole remarks back in late
August had sent yields soaring to multi-decade highs and unsettled
investors, but Wednesday's presser read as measured -- "coherent,
confident and consistently hawkish without coming across as crazily
so," per Evercore ISI's Krishna Guha. That clarity, not just the rate
move itself, is what let bond yields ease (30-year down to ~5.27% from
5.36%) and stocks rally. The Bank of England holding rates and
canceling a long-dated gilt sale added to the global yield relief.

UBS's Haefele summed up the bull case afterward: positioned for
further gains while bracing for near-term volatility, on the view
that if tightening stays measured and credit spreads hold, the rally
can broaden beyond the mega-cap names that carried it Thursday.

Source: [CNBC, Stocks surge as oil and bond yields retreat, recovering
from Fed-induced sell-off](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/16/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)

## 2026-09-02

Fed Governor Christopher Waller signaled support for no further rate
hike, which pulled Treasury yields down (10-year eased back from its
4.818% intraday high -- the highest since November 2023). That yield
relief was the direct trigger for the S&P 500 and Dow snapping their
three-day losing streak, closing higher as bond pressure eased.
Underlying tension remains: Iran conflict keeping oil elevated, and
market breadth deteriorating (S&P stocks above 50-day MA fell below
50% after topping 70% in mid-August) even as the index itself grinds
higher.
